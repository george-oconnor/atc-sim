How to read the radar

What every number on the screen means, and what you're actually meant to do with it. Read this once, then start Exercise 1.

← Exercises

The picture

A corridor of controlled airspace, walls on both sides. Aircraft fly through it. Nothing turns, climbs or descends unless you tell it to — a straight line at a fixed level, forever, until you say otherwise.

Your job for each one: keep it inside the walls, keep it 1,000 ft or 3 NM away from every other aircraft, and hand it off at the exit flight level printed on its label — the third row, left-hand number.

Reading an aircraft

Every aircraft on the radar is a small ring with a label stuck to it. The label is always the same three rows, in the same order:

  1. 1The ring — where it actually is, right now.
  2. 2History dots — where it's been. Fading trail behind it.
  3. 3Leader line — where it'll be in one minute. Longer line, faster aircraft.
  4. 4Callsign — say the airline name, then every digit.
  5. 5Current flight level — where it is now. FL080 = 8,000 ft.
  6. 6Climbing / descending, and cleared level — the arrow shows which way; the number is where you told it to go.
  7. 7Exit flight level — where it must be when it leaves your airspace. This is the target.
  8. 8Speed — knots. You can't change this, only altitude and heading.

What the colours are telling you

The label isn't just data — it's actively telling you what still needs doing.

  • amber cleared FLstill climbing or descending to get there — not there yet.
  • grey cleared FLarrived. Current and cleared level now match.
  • amber exit FLnot at its exit level yet — you still have work to do.
  • green exit FLat its exit level. Leave it there and it'll score correctly.
  • amber callsignclose enough to another aircraft to be worth watching.
  • red callsignseparation is actually lost — under 1,000 ft and under 3 NM from another aircraft.
  • orange callsignoutside the corridor walls.
  • white ringselected — this is the aircraft your next transmission will go to.

The strip panel

The same information again, listed down the right-hand side — easier to scan when you have several aircraft at once.

AAL451American four five one exit 220
080↑200 H070 251
  • aCurrent → cleared — same as the radar label's row 2.
  • bHeading — which way it's pointed, three digits.
  • cSpeed — knots.
  • dExit — the target level, top right. Turns green once reached.

Giving instructions

There are exactly three. Callsign first, then the instruction, every number said digit by digit — never "seventy", always "seven zero".

Turn“American four five one, turn right heading zero seven zero”
Climb“Delta six six, climb to flight level two eight zero”
Descend“United eight seven, descend to flight level two seven zero”
Correctionsaid wrong? Restate it: “… correction, United eight seven, descend to flight level two six zero”

What you're actually trying to do

Every aircraft that enters has to leave again, at its exit level, without ever getting too close to another one or wandering outside the walls. That's the whole job.

  • +100left at its exit flight level
  • −40left at the wrong level
  • −60outside the corridor walls, per excursion
  • −80never recovered, or left backwards
  • −150loss of separation, per pair
  • −5each phraseology fault
